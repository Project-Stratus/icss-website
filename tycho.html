Project Tycho

A high-thrust liquid bipropellant rocket engine

Project Tycho is a team of around 20 undergraduate, masters and PhD students designing, building and firing a liquid rocket engine. Tycho is the successor to Project Kepler, so you'll still see the Kepler name crop up in older documents and posts.

What we're working towards:

  • breaking the UK student record for the highest thrust produced by a liquid bipropellant engine (10 kN)
  • being the first student team to test a liquid bipropellant engine that uses turbomachinery designed in-house

Our hot-fire at Race 2 Space in July, in the Airborne Engineering test bays at Westcott Venture Park

The engine

PropellantsLiquid oxygen + isopropyl alcohol (IPA)
Thrust~11 kN (about 1120 kg) at 60 bar chamber pressure
CycleGas-generator (open) cycle
InjectorCoaxial swirl
CoolingLOx regen + film cooling
Other featuresSupersonic turbomachinery, autogenous re-pressurisation

The main changes from the last design are that it's manufactured in fewer parts, uses a new 24 kRPM turbopump, and has a longer chamber with a shorter nozzle.

What you'll learn

Over the year you'll get involved with CAD, FEA and CFD, regenerative cooling analysis, injector and nozzle design, turbomachinery, and machining and metal additive manufacturing, as well as writing test procedures and going through design reviews (PDR/CDR). There's also a fair bit of electronics work on the flow control unit and pressure sensors.

For new members we run a Rocket Engine Design Crash Course - over 4 weeks you design your own engine using the same theory and software that we use for the real one. It's a good place to start if you've never touched a rocket engine before (most of us hadn't either).

What we've done

  • Hot-fired the engine for the first time at Race 2 Space - it held together and produced 4500 N of thrust (enough to lift a grand piano)
  • Tested our turbine as part of a final year project, which confirmed the design works well
  • Passed our CDR and wrote all of the safety and testing procedures
  • Designed a new integrated chamber and nozzle and a new injector, and manufactured the turbomachinery
  • Submitted the PDR for the Tycho thrust chamber to Race 2 Space

Next up is turbopump testing at the start of term, then more hot-fires later in the year, all building towards a full integrated test of the new engine.
