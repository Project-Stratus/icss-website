The aim is to get the rocket as high as we can, record the flight with our own sensors, and get it back in one piece so it can fly again. ICSS ran this project until 2023 and it's restarting this year, led by Thomas van Pul and Curtis.

The last flight (2023)

The 2023 team flew a G-class rocket to 721 m. It carried a flight computer they designed and soldered themselves, with GPS, a LoRa radio, pressure, temperature and humidity sensors and an IMU, and it sent back telemetry all the way down. The rocket was recovered; the nose cone took a knock. The flight computer design and code are on GitHub.

This year

We're starting a new rocket: design it in OpenRocket and CAD, build it, test it on the ground, then fly it. We're sticking to G-class motors. The plan, the budget and the launch site are still being worked out, and we'll share them in the group chat as they're settled.

What you'll learn

The project covers a bit of everything: aerodynamics, electronics, mechanics, structures and mechanisms. You'll get experience with OpenRocket, MATLAB, CAD, FEA and CFD, as well as manufacturing through laser cutting, 3D printing and assembly.
