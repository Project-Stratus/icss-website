Balloon flights to Poland and Germany

August 17, 2019 · posted by Medad Newman

from the archive

On 5th of August 2019 we launched our most successful flights so far, both in terms of distance and flight duration. ICSPACE13 travelled over 1200km to Poland, flying over the Netherlands, Germany. Flight time was 13h 2min. ICSPACE12 and 13 were launched around 5.30pm on the 5th of August from our usual launch site at Wormwood Scrubs Space Port. The balloons traveled in 5 hours to the Netherlands and were picked up by a number of Dutch amatuer radio listeners who decoded the telemetry from our balloons. At around 11pm the balloons passed into Germany, breaking our distance record. At this point, the balloons were going out of range of the Dutch listeners on the West coast of the Netherlands. There are very few German listeners who usually listen in to telemetry from the UKHAS style transmissions. That was likely the last we heard from the balloons before they ran out of battery. It was already late night and no one was likely to wake up to track our balloons.

Remarkably, a Polish amatuer radio listener, SP3MCY, tuned in at 05:09h local time (03:09h UTC) and tracked ICSPACE13 for more than 2 hours as it flew deep into Poland. Many many thanks for your help. I suspect it went out of range of SP3MCY but must have carried on transmitting for some more time. Total flight time for ICSPACE13 was the longest so far, at 13h 2 min.

Key improvements

This time we filled the resealable valves of both balloons with Super glue. This seemed to make a massive difference to the resilience of the balloons. The resealable valves on these qualatex balloons are designed to self seal after filling. However, it looks like they leak under high pressure. ICSPACE12 was quite a heavy tracker, weighing in at 20.7g. In past launches we have seen balloons with heavy trackers like these burst prematurely at around 5000-6000m. Remarkably, ICSPACE12 made it all the way to 7880m altitude and later entered a float. Thanks to Mikael Dagman SA6BSS for that idea. The weakest point in the qualatex balloons are the resealable valves. Supergluing them together after filling seems to make a big difference.

We planned to remove the LEDs on the BN220 GPS modules we use. We found out that the blinking indicator LEDs consume up to 10mA@3.3V. We thought removing the LEDs would increase the battery life by several hours. However, during assembly after removing the LEDs, we found that the GPS module for ICSPACE12 would not get a fix anymore. In the last minute we swapped out the GPS with another one. LEDs were not removed and the heavy ceramic antenna left on. According to Richard, the removal of the surface mount LEDs was such an invasive process that it may have damaged the GPS. ICSPACE13 was launched without the LEDs and was still transmitting over 13h, the longest we have see it work.
