Imperial Planetary Robotics Lab

Run with the Imperial College Robotics Society (ICRS)

IPRL is a student-led initiative with a mission to "give students the space to gain experience in space robotics". That can mean rovers, drones or even (one day) mini robot swarms. At the moment there are three branches:

  • Harbinger - the rover team, competing in the European Rover Challenge
  • Triton - the research branch
  • Alumni Project - which brings ex-IPRL members and other alumni together to keep building

European Rover Challenge

The European Rover Challenge is held in Poland every year, and last year we placed 16th out of more than 100 teams registered worldwide. This year the goals are to rebuild the robotic arm, implement autonomous driving and get into the top 10. The competition itself is in Kraków.

The competition is split into several tasks: maintenance (adjusting switches and plugging in cables using the arm), probing (picking up a probe and dropping it into the payload), navigation (an autonomous drone and rover), science (drilling and sample collection, plus some astrobiology) and a presentation.

The rover team is split into electronics, drive/chassis/suspension, robotic arm, drill and payload, science, and UAV. It's a lot of fun, but it does take up some time, so ideally you'd be able to put in around 4-8 hours a week.

Triton (research)

In the first term you'll do an introduction to planetary research through three small projects (sample collection on Mars, the geology of Venus, and using lunar water for a permanent moon base). The second term is a larger project, supervised by a professor, with the aim of publishing the work. Team associates commit around 3-4 hours a week and team leads around 4-6.

What you'll learn

Depending on which sub-team you join, you'll work on robotic arm design, drive, chassis and suspension, electronics, UAV autonomy, autonomous navigation (including ArUco marker localisation), and drilling and sample payloads. The science side covers some astrobiology, and on the research branch you'll learn how to carry out and write up real research. There's also lots of project management along the way.
